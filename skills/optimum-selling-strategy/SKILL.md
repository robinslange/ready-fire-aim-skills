# Optimum Selling Strategy (OSS)

From Michael Masterson, *Ready, Fire, Aim* (2008), ch 4-8.

**Core principle:** for any business at any time there is one best way to acquire customers, and in the first stage it's the way that creates cash flow. The only deadly business problem is failing to bring in new customers. Until the OSS is found, about 80% of time and money goes to finding it.

An OSS has four keys, and the lock needs all four. They're ranked by how much each decides the result:

| # | Key | Question | Default answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Media** | Where are the customers, and where do they already buy things like this? | Where competitors advertise *repeatedly*. A mediocre pitch in the right place makes money, and a great pitch in the wrong place gets nothing. |
| 2 | **Product** | What do you sell them *first*? | A version of what is already selling to them, improved where the incumbents fall short. Keep a second candidate ready. |
| 3 | **Price** | What do you charge? | The industry norm. Test higher only once baseline numbers exist. Lean low on the lead product to reach critical mass, because profit comes from what they buy next. |
| 4 | **Copy** | How do you persuade them? | The copy platform below. This is the one place to be original. |

**Imitate the industry norm** on media, product, price and terms, and differ on **one** element: a channel you own, a pricing structure nobody else offers, or a sharper USP. Originality everywhere at once is untestable.

## Procedure

1. **Media map.** List where the category's buyers are reached today: directories, marketplaces, search terms, associations, events, referral sources, the places competitors return to. Ask competitors and adjacent vendors directly ("15 minutes, I'm learning the space"). Tally frequency. Repeat placements are proven.
2. **Product pick.** Name the 3-5 offers that already sell to this buyer. For each, list features, benefits and shortcomings. Every shortcoming is an opening. Choose the lead product, plus a backup.
3. **Price.** Find the norm (published prices, competitor quotes, what the buyer pays today for the manual way). Start there. Halving the price roughly doubles response, so a low lead price buys customers faster. Don't confuse a high price with value.
4. **Copy platform** (3-4 pages, written before any copy):
   - **Features → benefits → deep benefit.** For each benefit, ask "and why does that matter?" until you hit a concrete fear or fantasy. No abstractions: "success" must become money, time back, an evening with the kids, not looking foolish.
   - **USP**, passing three tests: it *appears* unique, it is *useful* to the buyer, and it is *simple* to say. Template: "the only [category] that [differentiator]." A preemptive USP claims a trait every competitor shares and none has said out loud.
   - **Big Idea** (from the USP; must excite *you* first, then check it excites the buyer) → **Big Promise** (headline and lead) → **specific claims** (brainstorm freely, then make each one concrete: "save time" becomes "get Friday afternoons back") → **proof** for every claim (case, number, testimonial, demo). Cut any claim you can't prove.
5. **Targets.** Pick two numbers: **CMQC**, the critical mass of qualified customers (buyers who'll buy again) that makes later sales profitable, and **AAC**, the allowable acquisition cost, meaning the most you can spend (money and hours) to win one. Judge the strategy by these two, not by revenue.
6. **Test.** Use direct response (a real ask, a measurable reply) across several media, 2-3 prices and at least 2 genuinely different copy platforms. Pre-sell or dry-test before building or stocking anything. Real purchases beat surveys and focus groups.
7. **Run the winner** until it stops working, then go back to step 1.

## Output

A one-page OSS sheet: media (ranked, with evidence), lead product and backup, price and its source, USP, Big Idea, Big Promise, top 3 claims with proof, CMQC, AAC, and the test matrix (media × price × copy, with the stop-loss per cell). Mark every field as **known** (evidence cited) or **guess**. The guesses are what to test first.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rewriting positioning when the channel is wrong | Media is key #1. Change *where* before *what you say*. |
| Selling the company (brand, logo, about page) | In stage one, sell the product. Nobody buys the company. |
| Rational spec-list pitch for a want | Buying is emotional first. Ladder benefits to the feeling. |
| "Unique" that isn't useful, or useful that takes a paragraph | Apply all three USP tests. |
| Copy with claims and no proof | The founder audits proof, because writers skip it. |
| Original on every axis | Imitate the norm, differ on one. |

Once copy exists, grade it against the copy platform (or with a copy-review skill such as [copy-chief](https://github.com/robinslange/copy-chief)). Use `ready-fire-aim` for running the test and the kill/continue decision.
