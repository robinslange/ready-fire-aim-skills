# Selling to Existing Customers

From Michael Masterson, *Ready, Fire, Aim* (2008), ch 9, 10, 15, 16, and Jay Abraham's three levers.

**Core principle:** revenue grows three ways. You sell to more people, sell more to the people you have, or charge more. The second is the cheapest. Existing customers already trust you, respond at much higher rates and will pay more. Acquiring a customer is often breakeven at best. The profit is in the **back end**, meaning what they buy next.

## Rules

1. **No dead ends.** Every sale leads to a next offer. After each delivery, ask where the customer is now and what they'd want next, then offer it.
2. **Sell the best customers a higher version** of what they already bought: more, better, closer. Masterson sold $2K-conference buyers a $10K "more and better" version, and 30 seats sold out in weeks.
3. **Back-end prices run higher.** Front end gets the customer; back end is priced to what the work is worth to them. Don't carry an acquisition discount into a renewal.
4. **The top ~20% give ~80% of profit.** Treat them as VIPs: more contact, first access, a personal tone.
5. **Strike while they're warm.** The best moment to offer the next thing is right after a purchase or a win, before they cool off.
6. **Know what they really want.** That's the deep desire, not the product category. Don't ask "why do you buy from us?" because people don't answer that honestly. Look at what they actually asked for and paid for.
7. **Stay consistent with the relationship.** Never offer anything (discounts, off-brand work) that undercuts the reputation you're building.
8. **Test new products on existing customers first.** Offer free or early access in exchange for feedback. If it doesn't sell to people who trust you, it won't sell to strangers, and if it does, you get testimonials.

## Procedure

1. List customers by what they paid in the last 6 months. Mark the top 20%.
2. For each top customer: what they bought, what they've asked for since (unprompted asks are the strongest signal), what their next problem is, and what a "more and better" version would be.
3. Write **one** next offer per top customer, with scope, price and why now. Price it from value to them, not from your hours.
4. If a deal's terms are below what the work is worth (open scope, rev-share at a sub-floor effective rate), put the restructure on the calendar at the natural renewal point. Bring the new offer, not a complaint.
5. Make the next offer at the next warm moment (a delivery, a good month, a thank-you).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Chasing strangers while a happy customer keeps asking for more | Their asks are your back-end product line. Price them. |
| Open-ended scope with a fixed small fee | Define the next offer as a priced package. |
| Waiting for the customer to propose | No dead ends: you make the next offer. |
| Discounting to keep a good customer | Add value instead. The best customers want more, not cheaper. |
