# Business Stage Check

From Michael Masterson, *Ready, Fire, Aim* (2008), ch 1-3, 9, 19, 22, 24.

**Core principle:** what a business needs depends less on its industry than on its size. Each stage has one main problem, one challenge and one opportunity. Name exactly one of each. A list of twelve priorities means the stage hasn't been diagnosed.

## The four stages

| Stage | Revenue (USD in book) | Main problem | Challenge | Opportunity | Founder skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Infancy | 0 → 1M | You don't yet know how to sell it | The first *profitable* sale, made repeatable | Keep selling to a critical mass of qualified customers | Selling + getting things going |
| 2 Childhood | 1M → 10M | Breaking even; lead product exhausting its market | Many new profitable products, fast | Back-end products to existing customers; innovation × speed | Steady stream of ideas, tested fast |
| 3 Adolescence | 10M → 50M | Systems strained and customers notice | Chaos to order | Protocols, professional ops, 3 numbers per manager | Managing managers |
| 4 Adulthood | 50M+ | Growth stalls; founder is the sole innovator | Becoming entrepreneurial again | Intrapreneurs run profit centers | Investor, not operator |

For a solo business, scale the thresholds to its own target. The *ordering* of problems still holds: no stage-3 process work before a stage-1 sale is repeatable. Signs that stage 1 is over: you know where customers come from, what they pay and what it costs to win one, and sales arrive without heroics. That's an OSS (see `optimum-selling-strategy`). Signs that stage 2 is due: the lead product's sales flatten despite tweaking.

## Procedure

1. **Gather facts**, not impressions: revenue last 4 weeks, paying customers, new customers this period, open conversations, overdue follow-ups, last new sale date. Pull them from the CRM, invoices and memory files.
2. **Name the stage** and the one problem, challenge and opportunity, each in a sentence.
3. **Time audit.** Estimate last week's split: selling (asks, follow-ups, demos for named prospects, outreach) versus everything else. Stage 1 target is about **80% selling**. Report the real number.
4. **Founder bottleneck.** More than about an hour a day on work someone or something else could do (QC, admin, re-checking) means you are the bottleneck. Name what to stop, automate or hand off.
5. **Three numbers.** Pick the three numbers that show whether this stage's challenge is being met, and track only those. Stage 1 default: *new customers won*, *cost (money + hours) per customer won*, *qualified conversations opened*.
6. **One action for this week** that moves the challenge, with a date.

## Output

```
Stage: <n> — <one-line why>
Problem / Challenge / Opportunity: <one sentence each>
Selling share last week: <x>% (target ~80% in stage 1)
Bottleneck: <what to stop or hand off>
Three numbers: <name = value> ×3
This week: <one action, dated>
Parked: <work that is real but belongs to a later stage>
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Stage-3 fixes (process, dashboards, org design) in stage 1 | Park them. Structure is a stage-3 cure. |
| Starting a second product before the first sells repeatably | Sell one product extremely well first. |
| Settling into "self-employment": cutting costs when the lead product stalls | Stage 2 needs new products and back-end offers, not thrift. |
| Twelve priorities | One problem, one challenge, one opportunity. |
